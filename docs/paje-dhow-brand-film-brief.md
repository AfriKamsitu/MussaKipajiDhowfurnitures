# Paje Dhow Furniture — brand film direction

Status: production brief and generation prompt, not a rendered video. Based on the user's furniture direction. Proposed edit length: 90–120 seconds in landscape 16:9; duration and format are editorial suggestions, not supplied requirements.

## Creative intent

The viewer should immediately understand: “This beautiful furniture was handcrafted in Zanzibar from timber with history.”

Show the same identifiable reclaimed dhow timbers progressing from rough stock through skilled workshop work to finished furniture in a Zanzibar villa. Premium quality comes from the craftsmanship, history, material and lasting construction. The furniture remains the subject; architecture, landscape, craftspeople and styling support its story.

Brand character: authentic, handcrafted, Zanzibar, reclaimed dhow wood, rustic, premium, timeless, coastal African.

## Furniture requirements

### Hero coffee table — inspired by Coffee Table Udzungwa

A substantial reclaimed-timber coffee table with thick square wooden legs, a heavy timber frame, visible joints and an inset glass center. Preserve aged grain, small cracks, old nail holes and natural variation. Give the glass real thickness, reflections, a supported edge and a plausible fit within the timber surround.

The reference product has a glass top above a compartmented drawer. Show that construction where it is visible; the frame and timber should dominate over decorative contents. Avoid turning it into a thin-legged glass table or replacing the glass with a solid slab.

### Hero wine rack — inspired by Wine Rack Kigoma

A sturdy rustic reclaimed-wood unit with open bottle compartments, simple shelves and small functional drawers. Use substantial framing, aged edges, visible joinery and mixed natural wood tones. Bottles must sit securely within real compartments. Preserve its handcrafted, multipurpose character; avoid a steel-grid rack or glossy fitted kitchen unit.

### Complementary furniture

Include thick-topped dining tables, wooden benches, reclaimed dhow-wood chairs, console tables, side tables, bookshelves, outdoor lounge furniture, wooden cabinets, bed frames and sofas with substantial exposed reclaimed-wood frames and natural cream cushions. Apply the same timber character and construction language to every piece, including background furniture.

## Material and continuity rules

- Keep old nail holes, small stable cracks, uneven grain, weathered edges, natural color variation, dark marks and previous joinery marks legible in both workshop and lifestyle shots.
- Remove loose splinters and soften contact edges while retaining the historical surface. Finished furniture must look comfortable, stable and usable.
- Sand selectively. Apply natural oil to deepen grain with a matte or restrained low-sheen finish; never erase the timber's character.
- Choose a distinctive cluster of nail holes and a dark grain mark on the hero table. Track those same features through selection, assembly, finishing and the final villa scene.
- Keep table proportions, leg thickness, joint positions, drawer divisions and glass shape consistent across shots. Keep the wine rack's compartment layout consistent.
- Establish approved product reference stills before generating motion. Use the same references for each view of a hero piece.
- Treat generated scenes as illustrative brand imagery, not documentation of a real workshop visit, installation or exact catalog item. Do not add unverified client names or product claims.

## Shot sequence

1. **The timber's first life.** Begin close to weathered dhow planks with old fastener holes and previous joints. A brief coastal view establishes Zanzibar, then return immediately to the timber. Salt-weathered character should be visible without making the usable wood appear rotten.
2. **Selection.** Craftsmen lay out reclaimed planks in a working Zanzibar workshop. Hands trace the grain and select the recognizable board that will become part of the hero table.
3. **Sanding macro.** Close view of careful sanding, dust and light catching the grain. Show roughness becoming touchable while nail holes and old markings remain.
4. **Joinery macro.** A craftsman marks and cuts a credible timber joint, then test-fits it. Tools contact the wood correctly; wood shavings and hand movements follow the action.
5. **Thick leg assembly.** Show a square table leg fitting into the substantial frame. A slightly wider angle establishes all four legs and the table's weight and stability.
6. **Glass fitting macro.** Show the completed timber surround and compartmented drawer, followed by a real glass panel carefully lowered onto its prepared supports. Use appropriate handling protection. The glass remains a rigid object throughout the action.
7. **Wine rack construction.** Show bottle-storage dividers being fitted into the frame, then simple shelves and small drawers being assembled. Include a wider angle so the finished compartment layout is clear.
8. **Natural oil and hand finish.** Macro view of oil wiped along the timber, darkening grain subtly. Match to the known nail-hole cluster after finishing. Finish with a hand checking a softened edge.
9. **The completed pair.** Brief clean workshop views of the coffee table and wine rack establish that these are the same objects about to appear in the villa.
10. **Villa living space.** A slow, low camera move centers the glass-inset coffee table between rustic timber sofas with cream cushions. Keep the thick legs, visible joints and grain in focus. Natural fabrics and the room remain quiet supporting elements.
11. **Wine rack portrait.** Frame the handcrafted rack against a warm lime-plastered Swahili-style wall with tropical plants nearby. Move from the entire piece to a small drawer, aged timber edge and bottle compartments. Plants must not conceal its construction.
12. **Dining and circulation.** Reveal a thick-topped dining table with reclaimed-wood chairs and a wooden bench in an open-air dining space. Continue to a console placed naturally along a coral-stone or plastered passage.
13. **Rooms made to live in.** Give separate readable views to a side table, a bookshelf, a wooden cabinet and a substantial timber bed frame. Reuse the same restrained material palette; avoid cramming unrelated objects into one shot.
14. **Outdoor living.** Show sturdy reclaimed-wood lounge furniture in a shaded veranda or courtyard with palms and cream cushions. Let warm sunlight rake across a timber armrest. A subtle Indian Ocean view sits behind the furniture.
15. **Final hero.** Return to the coffee table and timber sofas. The camera settles where glass, heavy frame, old grain marks and cream cushions can all be read. Hold long enough for the craft to register.
16. **End card.** Finish with the exact brand message below. Use clear Alibaba Sans typography added during editing. Allow a calm readable hold; do not rely on an image or video model to spell the text.

## Zanzibar setting and cinematography

Use white or warm lime-plastered walls, coral-stone details, tropical plants, palms, natural woven fabrics, open-air spaces and warm sunlight. Keep Indian Ocean glimpses subtle. Architecture should read as a lived-in Zanzibar coastal villa, with Swahili design cues rather than a generic luxury resort.

Use realistic material response, warm restrained color, slow tracking moves, deliberate cuts and tactile macro photography. Wide shots should still give the furniture enough image area to show its construction. Avoid excessive shallow focus that hides the defining joints or compartments.

Sound direction: gentle coastal ambience, cloth against wood, sanding, a restrained tap as a joint seats and quiet workshop activity. If music is used, keep it understated and secondary to the making. No voiceover is required by this brief.
