## Product and material continuity

**Coffee table:** an Udzungwa-inspired rectangular coffee table with four thick square reclaimed-wood legs, substantial apron and timber surround, visible wood joints and a supported inset glass center over a compartmented drawer. The glass remains transparent and rigid; it does not cover or replace the heavy timber surround. Keep leg proportions, drawer divisions, glass shape, nail-hole clusters and grain marks identical across views.

**Wine rack:** a Kigoma-inspired rustic reclaimed-wood unit with open horizontal bottle-storage compartments, simple shelves and small drawers. It is a substantial handmade piece with credible bottle support, aged edges and visible joinery, never a thin metal wine grid.

**Other furniture:** thick dining tables, timber chairs and benches, console and side tables, bookshelves, cabinets, bed frames, outdoor lounge pieces and sofas with thick exposed reclaimed-wood frames and cream cushions. The same material language applies to all background furniture.

**Timber history:** show old nail holes, small stable cracks, uneven grain, weathered edges, natural color variation, dark marks, old paint remnants and previous joinery marks. Smooth contact edges and remove splinters while preserving these features. Natural oil may deepen the color but must not produce glossy new-looking wood.

**People and place:** maintain the same adult Zanzibari craftspeople, practical contemporary work clothes, workshop layout, timber stacks, benches and lighting direction. Show people as skilled professionals. Use modern tools selectively alongside hand tools; no factory production lines or costume styling.

**Villa:** white or warm lime-plastered walls, coral-stone details, natural fabrics, tropical plants, palms, open-air circulation and warm sunlight. Use subtle ocean views and restrained styling. Architecture supports the furniture.
